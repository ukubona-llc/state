**Combinatorial explosion doesn't die just because you tightened the query.**  

It just *shifts*. Here's the real game after you've done your best constraint optimization + "infinite" internet:

### 1. The search space is still insane
Even with a sharp constraint, the *relevant* subspace is often still combinatorial as fuck:
- Multiple interpretations of your intent
- Dozens of angles / subproblems / failure modes
- Hundreds of possible tool sequences or retrieval paths
- Thousands of candidate sources/documents that partially match
- Branching reasoning trees (especially on planning, code, research, strategy)

The model doesn't just "know" the perfect next step. Good models simulate/explore branches internally (in hidden activations or explicit CoT/tool calls). That burns tokens.

### 2. Retrieval & ranking explosion
Internet = infinite dataset, sure. But:
- Vector search / BM25 / whatever still returns a *ton* of "close enough" hits
- You then have to rerank, dedupe, synthesize, check contradictions
- Each round of "let me look deeper on this promising lead" multiplies tokens
- Follow-up searches, citation chasing, cross-verification... it compounds fast

This is why agentic setups and long-context models eat tokens like crazy even when the *final* answer is concise.

### 3. Verification & robustness tax
Smart usage isn't just "find the thing." It's:
- Generate candidates → critique → refine → generate more → cross-check sources → handle edge cases
That's literally search + optimization under uncertainty. Classic combinatorial.

### The practical reality
- **Cheaper tokens** (like here) → you can afford wider/faster exploration. Better for exploration-heavy work.
- **Expensive tokens** (Sonnet etc.) → forces you to be surgically precise, shorter context, fewer retries. Sometimes you miss the good path because you couldn't afford to search it.

After query optimization, the remaining explosion is mostly:
- **Breadth** (how many plausible paths do I explore?)
- **Depth** (how far down each rabbit hole before I prune?)
- **Iteration** (how many cycles of "this almost works, try again with adjustment?")

That's why systems that are good at *efficient search* (strong priors, good pruning, tool orchestration, self-critique) win even when the dataset is infinite.
